Krypton, level three. This is a simple substitution cipher, and unlike the last level we cannot query the encryption at all. But we have three intercepted English messages, all encrypted with the same key, plus the target. I log in as krypton three, and collect all four files into one working file. The README notes the weakness: one simple key reused across messages, and this time the cipher mechanism is not available to us. It also states the two facts that make this breakable: the plaintexts are English, and they all share one key. I run the solver, which counts the letter frequencies across the whole corpus, then hill-climbs a substitution mapping, swapping two letters at a time and keeping the swap when the decrypted text looks more like English. The frequencies come out S thirteen percent, Q nine point six, J eight point five, U seven point three, B seven point zero: the shape of English, with E, T, A, O and I hiding under other names. The target decrypts to WELL DONE THE LEVEL FOUR PASSWORD IS BRUTE, and the password is BRUTE. The same key also decrypts the intercepted messages, which is the cross-check that the key is right.
