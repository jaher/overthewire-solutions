Bandit, level eleven. The password in data dot txt has been rotated by thirteen positions, the classic ROT thirteen cipher. Every letter is shifted halfway around the alphabet, and because thirteen is half of twenty-six, applying the same shift again returns the original text. It is its own inverse. The tr command is perfect here: we map the uppercase range A to Z onto N to Z then A to M, and the lowercase range the same way. Piping the file through tr prints the readable sentence and the password for bandit twelve. We verify by logging in as bandit twelve. The lesson is that ROT thirteen is not encryption, just an encoding.
